What I learned

Where it connects to my work

Several parts of the course covered work I have done in my operations role, so it was useful to see the wider framework behind it.

  • Customer identification and due diligence - in my role I identified and classified entity account holders and looked through ownership structures to find the controlling persons.
  • KYC for different customer types - I handled trusts, charities, partnerships and nominee structures; the course explained why each type needs a different approach.
  • Sanctions and PEP screening - I saw screening hits and referred them to the specialist teams responsible for them; the course showed where this fits in a full AML programme.
  • Data privacy and security - I worked with sensitive customer records every day, so this chapter matched how I already had to work.

Already knew - now understand better

AML and KYC basics - the risk-based approach, ongoing monitoring and suspicious activity monitoring, plus how AML programmes are audited and reported.

New to me

How a bank makes moneyThe net interest margin: the gap between what a bank earns on loans and what it pays on deposits.
Asset-liability matchingWhy borrowing short-term and lending long-term is a core risk every bank has to manage.
Shadow bankingLenders that do bank-like work outside normal banking regulation.
Who oversees US banksThe roles of the Fed, the OCC and the FDIC, and how CCAR stress tests check whether a bank could survive a bad economy.
The insurance businessLife, health, property and casualty, and specialty insurers - and how insurers earn by investing premiums before claims are paid.
Insurance regulationThe NAIC and state-level rules, insurance reserves, and the rules for selling annuities.
IFRS 17The international accounting standard for how insurers report their contracts and profits over time.
